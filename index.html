The sheet

What each line is worth

This is the official evaluation sheet of the subject — the one that will be used on your own written project. Block A, appearance, is 15 % of the grade; block B, substance, is 85 %. Every section is scored from 0 to 10.

Block and sectionWeightWhat the evaluator asks
A · Appearance of the written project15 %Illustrations and captions · citations, correctly and consistently formatted · writing style · organisation and flow · concision · quality of the English.
B · Title2.5 %Is the title a specific summary of the proposed work?
B · Abstract2.5 %Does it give a brief summary of the proposal? Does it summarise the hypothesis and the goals?
B · Introduction20 %Enough background to educate the evaluator? Adequate review of the literature and technical detail? Does the text show understanding? Is the novelty or importance made clear?
B · Objectives20 %Do they define the scope? Is there a clear statement of the objectives, and of the hypothesis to be tested or the questions to be answered?
B · Methods + work plan + expected results40 %Described in adequate depth — thorough but not excessive? Is the approach plausible and adequate to the objectives? Is the management plan plausible, are the phases and the division of responsibilities adequate? Do they specify what they will deliver?
B · Budget15 %Properly written, distinguishing direct and indirect costs? Is every item justified? Do they have the resources to do it in the stated time and cost?
The arithmetic. Block B is the weighted average of its six sections. Final grade = 0.15 × A + 0.85 × B. The page does it live, but do it once by hand: it is the only way to feel how little the title is worth and how much the methods block is. The written project must reach 5, and it is 30 % of the mark of the subject.
Round 1 · 15 min

Score Draft A, on your own

Read it once straight through before you write a single number. Then score every line and write one sentence per section — that sentence is the comment box on the real sheet. No discussion yet: a committee member who has already heard someone else's mark cannot give an independent one.

Draft A · as submitted

Proposal to the committee

Title

Bats and light pollution.

Abstract

Light pollution is an increasing problem worldwide. This project will study the effects of artificial light on bats. Bats are important bioindicators of environmental quality. The results obtained will contribute to the knowledge of this subject and will be useful for managers and for society in general.

Introduction

Artificial light at night has increased greatly in recent decades and affects many organisms. Bats are nocturnal mammals and are therefore especially sensitive. Several authors have studied this question. In Mallorca there are many bat species and much of the island is illuminated. For all these reasons it is important to study this topic.

Objectives

1. To carry out acoustic surveys in illuminated and dark areas. 2. To analyse the data obtained. 3. To raise awareness among the public about light pollution.

Methods

Acoustic recorders will be placed in several locations of the island. The recordings will be analysed with specialised software. Statistical analyses will be performed in order to compare the different areas.

Work plan

The project will last 12 months. The team will carry out the fieldwork, the analysis and the dissemination.

Expected results

We expect to obtain interesting results about the effect of light on bats. The results will be published and presented at a congress.

Budget

Personnel 20 000 € · Equipment 10 000 € · Travel 5 000 € · Other expenses 5 000 € · TOTAL 40 000 €.

Round 2 · 10 min

In threes: find your widest disagreement

Put the three sets of scores side by side. A committee has to produce one mark, not three — notice what you had to give up to get there.

The section where you differed most
The single number you agreed on
The argument that changed someone's mind Was the spread caused by the proposal, or by the three of you?

Only one of those two is in the applicant's control. If the criterion was read differently, the fix is a clearer rubric; if the proposal was ambiguous, the fix is a clearer proposal.

Round 3 · 10 min

Now score Draft B, as a group

Same project, same team, same money, same twelve months. Only the writing is different. Score it line by line and note, in a few words, what moved it.

Draft B stays closed until Draft A has a mark on every line. The second half only works if you have not read it first.

Plenary · 12 min

The marks the committee gave

Say your two grades out loud first, and write the room's spread on the board. The spread is as interesting as the marks.

Opens once both drafts are scored.

SectionA BWhy
  1. Same project, same team, same money. On this sheet Draft A scores 2.9 and Draft B scores 7.4. Nothing about the science changed between them.
  2. The objectives moved from 2 to 8, and they are worth 20 % — one section worth more than a whole grade point of the final mark.
  3. Methods, work plan and expected results are 40 %: more than the introduction and the objectives together, and the section most people write last and shortest.
  4. Appearance is 15 % and it is the cheapest mark on the sheet — captions, consistent citations, nothing padded. Nobody should lose it.
